Getting started

With Bobin Kumanda you draw contactor control circuits as you would on paper, then watch how they really behave: press the buttons, coils pull in, motors turn and current flows through the wires. If something is wired wrongly the program tells you what happened and how to fix it.

  1. Open a ready-made circuit from File → Samples or the Samples button on the start screen (for example Latching).
  2. Start the simulation with F5.
  3. Click the buttons with the mouse. Hold a start button down, or Shift-click it to keep it pressed.
  4. The event log (bottom panel) shows the sequence: "S1 pressed → K1 pulled in → M1 running".
  5. Press F5 again to stop and change the circuit.

Contacts find their coil by label: every contact labelled K1 switches together with coil K1. Select a contact and change "Coil it belongs to" in the panel on the right.

Drawing

  • Adding components: drag them from the palette on the left, or click once and then click where it should go. Type "overload", "fuse", "motor" in the search box (Ctrl+F). Frequent ones have a single-key shortcut: K coil, A NO contact, C NC contact, B start, D stop, H lamp, M motor.
  • While placing: R rotates, F mirrors, Shift+click places several, Esc cancels.
  • Selecting and moving: click and drag. Drag from empty space for a rubber-band selection. Wires follow moved components at right angles. Arrow keys nudge by one grid step.
  • Labels: relays get K1, K2..., buttons S1, S2..., lamps H1... automatically (IEC). Drag a label to move it.
  • Properties: the panel on the right changes coil voltage, timer delay, motor power, lamp colour, breaker rating and more.
  • Undo: Ctrl+Z (200 steps), Ctrl+Y redo. Relays copied with Ctrl+C/V get new numbers and the contacts copied with them follow the new coil.
  • Sheets: + at the bottom adds a sheet. Use sheet connectors for long circuits: arrows with the same name are the same conductor.
  • Text: T adds a note.
  • Terminal numbers and cross reference: switch them on in the View tab. Auxiliary contacts are numbered in order (13-14, 21-22, 33-34...; timed contacts 15-16, 17-18) and every coil lists its contacts underneath.

Wires

  • Click a terminal (the dots at the ends of components), then click a second terminal. The wire is routed at right angles automatically.
  • Clicking empty space adds a corner; double-click ends the wire there.
  • Connecting to the middle of a wire creates a junction.
  • Drag a segment of a selected wire to adjust its path.
  • Wires take the colour of their phase: L1 brown, L2 black, L3 grey, N blue, PE green-yellow.
  • Loose wire ends are marked with a red ring.

Simulation

The program solves the circuit like a real electrical circuit: every voltage and every wire current is calculated. Coils pull in at 85% of their rated voltage and drop out below 55%. Short circuits, wrong voltages and missing phases follow from the calculation by themselves.

Instruments

Multimeter

  • Open it with Measure → Multimeter. Attach the red (V/Ω) and black (COM) probes in turn by clicking a terminal, junction or wire. Drag the instrument by its title to move it.
  • V~ AC voltage and V= DC voltage are measured while the simulation runs.
  • Ω resistance is measured with the circuit switched off (while editing): see the resistance of a coil or lamp and whether contacts are open or closed (continuity). An open circuit reads "OL".
  • Trying to measure resistance on a live circuit gives a warning: a real meter would be damaged.

Clamp meter

  • Open it with Measure → Clamp meter and click the wire whose current you want to see.
  • MAX holds the highest value: clamp it before starting a motor to see the starting current (5-6 times the rated current). Compare it with star-delta starting.

Protection and faults

  • Short circuit: an unprotected short stops the simulation, the short-circuit path flashes red and the Problems tab explains why. Put a circuit breaker or fuse at the start of the circuit: it trips and the simulation continues.
  • Circuit breaker: B, C or D curve; trips with a delay on overload (thermal) and at once on a short circuit (magnetic). Click its lever to reset it.
  • Overload relay: watches the motor current. Set it to the motor's rated current. When it trips 95-96 opens and 97-98 closes. It cannot be reset until it cools down.
  • Residual current device: trips when the current leaving on the phases and returning on neutral differ by more than 30 mA. Add a "body leakage" fault to a motor (panel on the right) to try it. If the motor's PE terminal is not connected, a dangerous touch-voltage warning appears.
  • Burnt coil: a 230 V coil between two phases (400 V), or DC on an AC coil, burns out.
  • Missing phase: the motor cannot start, hums and heats up; without an overload relay it burns.
  • Clicking a problem in the Problems tab shows the component.

Files, export and printing

  • Circuits are saved as .bobin. Unsaved work is backed up every 2 minutes and offered back after a power cut.
  • Old EKTS files (.esc) open. Save as can also write .esc; components the old program does not have and extra sheets are left out, and the program lists them.
  • Document info: student name, number, class, school, teacher and date, shown in the title block at the bottom right of the page.
  • Export as image: the page with its title block as PNG or JPG (for homework and reports).
  • Print (Ctrl+P): A4 or A3 landscape, fitted to the page. For PDF choose the "Microsoft Print to PDF" printer.
